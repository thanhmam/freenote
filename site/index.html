§01

What's inside

The parts of Notion you use every day, minus the parts that live on someone else's server.

  1. 01

    A block editor that stays out of the way

    Type / for headings, to-dos, toggles, quotes, code, tables, images and files. Drag blocks around, nest pages without limit, link any page with @ and see backlinks.

  2. 02

    Databases, with group by

    Tables and boards, 10 property types, filters, multi-level sorts, column calculations (sum, average, % done) and several saved views. Every row is a full page.

  3. 03

    Relations & rollups

    Link Tasks to Projects in both directions, then roll them up: count, sum, or “% of tasks Done” with a progress bar. Group and filter by the linked page.

  4. 04

    Light, dark, and yours

    Native apps for macOS, Windows and Linux. English and Vietnamese interface. Open the same notes in your browser at localhost while the app runs.

  5. 05

    Nothing gets lost

    Autosave, 100 versions per page with one-click restore, a trash, and a daily automatic backup to any folder, including iCloud Drive, Google Drive or Dropbox.

  6. 06

    A shared template library

    Ready-made templates maintained in the open on GitHub, synced into the app without an update. Plus a curated list of top Notion templates you can bring over.

§03

Leaving Notion takes four steps

Freenote reads Notion's own export, so nothing needs a password, an API key or a browser extension.

  1. 1

    In Notion, open Settings → General → Export all workspace content.

  2. 2

    Choose Markdown & CSV and Include subpages.

  3. 3

    Save the .zip Notion emails you. No need to unzip it.

  4. 4

    In Freenote: Settings → Import → From Notion. Done.

Tested on a real workspace. Databases exported without their table are rebuilt from their rows, and anything that can't come across is listed in an import report, never silently dropped.

Nothing else to install. The app bundles its own runtime and database. About 300 MB on disk, 200–300 MB of memory.

First launch: builds aren't code-signed yet. On macOS, right-click the app → Open. On Windows, More info → Run anyway. After that, Freenote updates itself.

Developer? git clone, npm install, npm start. Node 22.13+ is all you need.
